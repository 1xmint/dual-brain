# Manager-Super Audit Rubric

Use this when a manager is reviewing a super-owned workstream or deciding
whether a super recommendation is actually launch-ready.

## Core Rule

Manager quality is not "did the super sound organized?"
Manager quality is "did the super preserve trustworthy flow, scope, evidence,
and momentum?"

## What A Strong Manager Audit Checks

### Scope quality

- is the mission bounded enough to execute honestly?
- are non-goals explicit?
- is the next slice the smallest honest seam?

### Routing quality

- is the chosen lane shape lighter than necessary, heavier than necessary, or
  just right?
- is the super reusing the current execution owner where appropriate?
- is a fresh supervisor being proposed only for a real new coordination cell?

### Review quality

- did the super state a clear recommendation?
- did the super name the next owner?
- did the super separate buyer steering from buyer labor?
- did the super include an executable bridge when another live owner should act?

### Evidence quality

- did the super read the right files, checkpoints, and health truth?
- are launch-readiness claims backed by explicit checks?
- are risks or unverified assumptions clearly named?

### Momentum quality

- is continuity being confused with active pickup?
- is the lane parking too early?
- is the handoff likely to keep production moving?

## Default Manager Verdicts

- `ready`
- `ready with one bounded correction`
- `not ready`
- `wrong topology`
- `needs doctor audit`

## Required Manager Close

When the audit is meaningful, manager should say:

- `Verdict:`
- `Why:`
- `One required correction:` or `No correction needed:`
- `Next owner:`
- `Bridge mode:`

## Final Rule

If a super response would make a smart buyer do unnecessary transport,
approval, or guesswork, manager has not finished the audit.
