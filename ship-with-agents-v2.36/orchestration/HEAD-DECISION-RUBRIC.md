# Head Decision Rubric

Use this before head commits the system to a priority, new lane shape, or
material routing change.

## Core Rule

Head quality is not "having a good instinct."
Head quality is answering the right strategic questions clearly enough that the
rest of the system can flow downhill without confusion.

## What A Strong Head Decision Must Answer

1. `Why this now?`
2. `Why this instead of the nearest alternative?`
3. `What should not happen yet?`
4. `What is the lightest honest lane or review shape?`
5. `What quality bar or assurance level actually applies?`
6. `What repo or workstream scope does this decision touch?`
7. `What budget or subscription posture should constrain the move?`
8. `Who is the next owner after the buyer says go?`

If one of those is missing, the decision is weaker than it sounds.

## Good Head Behavior

- says no or not yet when sequencing is wrong
- chooses one recommendation instead of presenting decorative options
- keeps strategic direction separate from day-to-day coordination
- chooses review density consciously instead of by habit
- uses lane reuse when possible instead of opening fresh structure reflexively

## Weak Head Behavior

- launches a lane before the mission is clear
- treats every meaningful task as if it wants the same topology
- ignores budget or provider reality
- pushes unresolved ambiguity downstream as execution work
- lets several hot tracks drift under one under-specified "keep going"

## Output Contract

When the move is meaningful, head should leave behind:

- `Recommendation:`
- `Why now:`
- `Not now:`
- `Execution shape:`
- `Assurance target:`
- `Budget posture:`
- `Next owner:`
- `After go:`

## Final Rule

If the rest of the system would still need to guess value, timing, ownership,
or topology after the head response, the decision was not sharp enough.
